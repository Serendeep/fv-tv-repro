Background

Function vectors (FVs)[1] add the mean outputs of a few causally chosen attention heads (by AIE) to the residual stream. Task vectors (TVs, θ)[2] copy one hidden state from a demonstration prompt into a zero-shot pass. Both papers say the vector carries the task.

Setup

  • GPT-J-6B, Llama-3.1-8B and 70B, Gemma-2-9b base and IT.
  • 16 word-pair tasks from [1]; first-token accuracy.
  • Random vectors and heads control FVs; shuffled labels control TVs.

Test 1: Exploratory grid

Scaling the Gemma FV up to 8×, moving it before the RMSNorm, or using 40 shared heads (closer to [1]) does not rescue it.

Limitations

  • Our recipes differ from [1] and [2] (FV: 10 heads per task, no ICL-correct filter; TV: Q:/A: prompts). The Gemma null is about our implementation.
  • Tests 2 and 3 use 6B–9B models only. Test 3 is TV-only.
  • CIs resample 8 tasks; test inputs are fixed.
  • Follow-ups were chosen after earlier results.

Test 3: Sentiment with arbitrary labels

Positive maps to A and negative to B, or the reverse, so label meaning can’t help[4]. ICL learns this. The TV doesn’t.

In 18 of 24 cases the patched model gives every review the same answer.

Shuffled θ still works

Shuffled labels keep about half the effect, consistent with task recognition[5,6]. A related task’s θ does almost nothing.

Conclusions

  1. Word-pair success does not imply broader transfer. On sentiment, TVs stay near chance on all four checkpoints.
  2. Controls need their own held-out layer. On Llama-8B, it removes the TV’s apparent win.
  3. Our FV works on GPT-J and Llama-8B but not on Gemma. The cause is open.
